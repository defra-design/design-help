# How the Defra Design Help Tool Works

This tool is a dedicated space for the Defra design community to connect, share skills, and find support. Whether you need a quick design review, long-term coaching, or just someone to bounce ideas off, this platform helps you find the right colleague.

## Key Features

### 1. Secure Community Access

To ensure this remains a safe and trusted space for internal work, access is restricted to **Defra employees only**.

- **Registration**: You must sign up using your `@defra.gov.uk` email address.
- **Privacy**: Profiles are only visible to other logged-in members of the community.

### 2. Creating Your Profile

Your profile is your "storefront" to the community. It allows you to showcase:

- **Core Skills**: What you are good at (e.g., Interaction Design, Content Strategy, User Research).
- **Offers of Help**: Specific ways you are willing to support others (e.g., "Reviewing prototypes", "Career mentoring").
- **Experience & Bio**: A brief introduction to your background and role.

### 3. Managing Your Availability

We know that work comes in waves. To manage expectations, you can set your current status:

- **Available**: You are open to requests for help.
- **Busy**: You are currently at capacity.
  - *Smart Feature*: If you mark yourself as "Busy", you can set a date for when you'll be free again (e.g., "Busy until March 1st"). The system will automatically show when you expect to return, helping colleagues plan when to reach out.

### 4. Finding Help

The "Browse" page allows you to search the entire directory of designers.

- **Search**: You can type keywords to find people with specific skills (e.g., "Accessibility", "GDS patterns").
- **Filter**: You can quickly see who is available right now versus who might be busy.

## Why use this tool?

Before this tool, finding a specific skill in the team often meant sending broad emails or asking around on Slack. This tool creates a searchable directory of our collective expertise, making it easier to:

- Onboard new starters by connecting them with mentors.
- Solve tricky design problems by finding a subject matter expert.
- Foster a culture of collaboration and peer support across different Defra projects.
